Claude Account & Acceptable Use Policy

To receive your SUNY Poly–provisioned Claude account for COM 216 / FYS 111, you agree to this statement — the class agreement for the account. It's short, and it says plainly what the account is, what happens to what you produce with it, and what choices you have.

What the account is

This is an institutional Claude account, issued to you for the duration of this course. It isn't your personal Claude account, and it isn't unlimited in scope — it's provisioned so you can complete the work assigned for this course, and that must be the top priority for your allotted tokens. Each person has a limited amount of tokens per four-hour session, so plan your coursework so it fits inside a single session, rather than running the budget out partway through Workbench work, Zotero-assisted research, or an observation checkpoint.

Outside of coursework, you're free to use this account in other parts of your life, within two limits:

  • Other coursework. If you use it for academic work in another class, that class's own AI-use policy or instructor guidance governs, not this one.
  • SUNY Poly's IT policy. Every use of this account, coursework or not, is still governed by SUNY Poly's Appropriate Use of Information Technology policy — the same policy that covers every other SUNY Poly IT system. That policy limits use outside your academic responsibilities to incidental personal use, not open-ended personal use, and it carries the ordinary obligations of any institutional account. See “Acceptable use” below.

What you're agreeing to

Signing this statement means you agree to the terms already described in This Is an Open Course, specifically:

  • Your weekly Workbench submission is scooped up and referenced the way the Workbench Sharing Policy describes, with no opt-out from that part. What you control is attribution: whether your specific contributions are called out to classmates by name, by pseudonym, or not at all. See that document for the full picture; you make the actual election as part of the Course Agreements quiz below.
  • You may submit under a pseudonym if you don't want classmates to know which workbench is yours. Course staff always know your real identity regardless of the name on the file.

If any of that is unfamiliar, read This Is an Open Course before signing — this statement is the agreement; that document is the explanation.

What's visible to the instructor, and what isn't

Because this is an institutional account issued for the course, your usage — sessions, activity, general behavior in the account — is visible to the instructor the same way your activity in Brightspace is.

Two different things follow from that:

  • Your individual-level account activity is treated as an education record: visible to the instructor and course staff for running the course, never shared with classmates, never published.
  • Aggregate, class-level patterns — what the group as a whole is doing with the model — are a separate matter, and are part of what this course studies. An observation about the class in aggregate is never the same disclosure as an observation about you specifically. There is no opt-out from this aggregate component — your workbench content contributes to it regardless of any choice you make about peer sharing. The opt-out described above governs only whether your specific contributions are called out by name or pseudonym to your classmates.

Acceptable use

This account is provisioned for COM 216 / FYS 111 coursework first, and that comes first for your token budget. Beyond coursework:

  • Personal use is allowed, but it has to stay incidental. This account is not a substitute for your own personal Claude account, and heavy non-coursework use crowds out the token budget the account exists to protect.
  • Use in another class is governed by that class's own AI-use policy or instructor, not by this document.
  • All use, without exception, is governed by SUNY Poly's Appropriate Use of Information Technology policy, available at sunypoly.edu/its/policies/appropriate-use-of-information-technology (external site). That policy is incorporated into this agreement by reference — read it in full before you sign. In brief, it covers: who it applies to (everyone using a SUNY Poly IT system, students included); what counts as inappropriate use (interfering with others' access, harassment, unauthorized access or impersonation, distributing malware, illegal use, and use for private commercial gain, among others); that access is a privilege the university can restrict or revoke; and that SUNY Poly can review account activity under specific conditions the policy lists (security or legal need, suspected policy violation, and similar). Nothing in this document narrows or waives that policy — where the two conflict, the SUNY Poly policy governs.

Agreement

By completing the acknowledgment described below, you confirm that you've read this statement, This Is an Open Course, and the Workbench Sharing Policy, and you make your attribution election — called out by name, by pseudonym, or not at all — as part of that same acknowledgment, without penalty to your grade regardless of which you choose.

Acknowledgment mechanism: complete the Course Agreements quiz in Brightspace after you've read this document. The quiz is release-conditioned on having visited this document, This Is an Open Course, the Workbench Sharing Policy, and the SUNY Poly Appropriate Use of Information Technology policy as Content topics. It asks you to acknowledge that you've read each one, and it's where you record your attribution election for the term.
