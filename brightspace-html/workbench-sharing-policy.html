Workbench Sharing Policy

Every week, you submit your Workbench as a zip file: transcripts, artifacts, and observations. This document describes what happens to that file after you submit it, and where the line sits.

What “scooped up” means

Course staff collect the week's Workbench zip files from the class and use them for two things:

  • Writing the provocation that opens the next observation checkpoint, drawing on patterns and specific moments from what the class actually did that week.
  • Referencing specific content from workbenches — a transcript excerpt, an artifact, an observation — directly in course materials, as an example of how the group is engaging with the model and the material.

There is no opt-out from this. Being enrolled means your Workbench content is scooped up and referenced this way; it's part of how the course runs, not an add-on you can decline while still completing the same assignments.

What you can control

You can't opt out of your content being scooped up and referenced. You can control whether you're identified when it is referenced — by your name, by a pseudonym, or not at all beyond “a student in the class.” That's the opt-out described in the Claude Account & Acceptable Use Policy: it governs attribution, not whether your content gets used.

The FERPA boundary

Referencing your Workbench content in course materials stays inside the class. Nothing from a Workbench zip is published, posted publicly, or shared with anyone outside the students and staff enrolled in this section of COM 216 / FYS 111. Using student work to run the course that work was produced for is the kind of internal educational use FERPA allows; publishing it outside the class would not be.

If a moment from your Workbench is ever wanted for something outside this class — a conference talk, a publication, a future course — that requires your separate, specific permission. This document doesn't cover that; it only covers what happens inside this section of COM 216 / FYS 111.
